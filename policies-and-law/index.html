Understand what you’re being told

A requirement and a price are different questions.

A funeral home’s explanation should help you understand both why something is needed and what you are paying for. You can ask for that explanation without accusing anyone of wrongdoing.

Who is setting the requirement?

A law or regulation

A government requirement may depend on the location, timing, and circumstances. Ask for the exact provision, which authority issued it, and why it applies to your arrangements. A verbal statement that “it’s the law” is a starting point for a question, not a citation.

A provider’s policy

A funeral home, crematory, cemetery, or other provider may set conditions for the services it offers. Ask for the written policy and available alternatives. A provider policy is not automatically a rule every other provider follows; its enforceability is a separate question.

A recommendation or preference

A provider may recommend a particular service or product. Ask about its purpose, the alternatives, and the consequences of declining it. A recommendation alone does not explain whether something is legally required.

Questions you can use for any charge.

Families may hear requirements about viewing, preparation, containers, transport, or cemetery services. Start with the same questions, then check the details for your circumstances.

“Who requires this, and can I see the written requirement? What options meet it? What does each option cost, and what is included?”

Even when an item or service is required, that alone does not establish that a particular product or price is required. Ask about acceptable alternatives and compare the full cost. A high price alone does not establish wrongdoing.

For an official starting point, see the FTC Funeral Rule consumer guide and FTC guidance for funeral providers. State-specific research is still pending.

Price includes more than the product.

Providers have staffing, facilities, equipment, transport, administrative, and other operating costs. A sustainable profit helps keep a business available to serve families. A markup or a price difference alone does not establish that a charge is unfair or unlawful.

Ask what is included, compare equivalent services, and consider the total cost. Our purpose is to support informed choices and accurate explanations, not to presume wrongdoing by a provider.

Keep a clear record.

  1. Write down the exact requirement you were told about.
  2. Ask for the law or written policy and an itemized price.
  3. Ask what alternatives are available and how their total costs compare.
  4. If the explanation remains unclear, contact the relevant state regulator with the specific question.
