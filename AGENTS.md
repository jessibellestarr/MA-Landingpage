# Morticians Audit: standing product and design instructions

Apply these instructions to every product, content, design, research, and monetization decision in this repository. These record the founder's requirements, not optional suggestions. Preserve them across future tasks.

## Purpose and audience

Build a practical consumer resource for funeral and cremation decisions, both after a death and when planning ahead. Some users will be grieving, under time pressure, financially constrained, or unfamiliar with funeral terminology. Do not assume everyone has the same emotional response or needs.

Support informed choices, accurate explanations, and respectful conversations. Recognize legitimate provider overhead and sustainable profit. Do not equate high prices or markups with wrongdoing, generalize negative experiences to the industry, or imply a legal finding. Founder experience is personal testimony, not research evidence or proof of current law. The founder formerly held a North Carolina funeral service license covering funeral directing and embalming; do not imply current licensure.

## Marketing and behavioral design

Act as an evidence-informed consumer product designer and ethical marketer, not only a copy editor. Before major design changes, identify the user's task, uncertainties, barriers, and useful outcome. Review relevant usability research and comparable consumer journeys when research access is available. Record sources, applicability, and limitations; do not claim research was performed when it was not.

The first screen must immediately explain what guidance is offered, who it helps, and what someone can do next. Prefer concrete funeral-planning and cost language over vague reassurance. Keep the voice warm, direct, calm, and non-accusatory.

Use progressive disclosure: ask only what is needed for the next useful step, explain why a question matters, provide an honest unsure option, and allow backtracking. Distinguish at-need from pre-need in plain language. Separate provider, service, death/transport-origin, and disposition locations; residence alone does not determine applicable requirements. Provide a direct browse route without forcing onboarding.

Reduce cognitive effort with descriptive headings, clear hierarchy, familiar controls, short explanations, and a clear primary next action. Avoid multiple competing starting points. Keep essential information available without an account. Evaluate mobile, keyboard, screen-reader semantics, contrast, error recovery, and no-JavaScript access where feasible.

The founder prefers golden-ratio-inspired spacing and proportions. Treat this as an aesthetic preference, not evidence that a ratio calms grief or improves conversion. Readability, accessibility, and usability take priority.

Never exploit grief or use shame, fear, false urgency, scarcity, hidden fees, preselected purchases, misleading testimonials, or manipulative defaults. Optimize first for comprehension and useful completed tasks, not clicks or purchases alone. Propose consent-respecting validation; do not add tracking without explicit authorization.

## Research, citations, and legal content

Every state should ultimately have genuinely researched guidance, not generic text presented as state-specific law. Cite primary official statutes, administrative regulations, and regulator guidance, with exact provisions and direct links where possible. Explain jurisdiction, scope, exceptions, and the practical question answered. Distinguish law, regulator interpretation, provider policy, operational requirements, and recommendations.

Maintain a research record for each substantive claim: source URL, section, relevant excerpt or faithful summary, date actually reviewed, applicability, uncertainties, and reviewer status. Do not fabricate citations or review dates. A page build/update date is not a legal verification date. Check sources before asserting facts; label unresolved or inaccessible material and avoid unsupported universal claims, especially about transport, embalming, viewing, and disposition.

Every public page must carry the shared information-can-change notice and links to relevant official sources where available. Disclaimers do not excuse inaccurate content. State research marked pending must remain pending until actual source review supports a change. Explain findings as educational information, not personalized legal advice, compliance certification, or accusations.

Behavioral and market claims require evidence too. Prefer original research, reputable usability studies, and relevant consumer research. Distinguish causation from correlation, acknowledge population/context limits, and treat untested application to this audience as a hypothesis. Competitor patterns are observations, not proof of effectiveness. Do not invent market size, conversion lifts, willingness to pay, or research results.

## Sustainable revenue

The goal is to cover operating costs by providing genuine optional value. Keep essential rights information and basic guidance free. Clearly distinguish available features from planned features and proposed prices from validated prices. Do not create fake checkout links or imply payments/delivery work before verifying them. Evaluate willingness to pay through ethical research rather than assuming distressed visitors will buy. Explain monetization and commercial relationships transparently.

## Execution and validation

Before a substantial change, briefly record the intended user benefit, evidence or hypothesis, chosen approach, and how it will be checked. Keep this proportional; small copy fixes need no elaborate research exercise. For major journeys, use representative scenarios including uncertainty, multiple states, direct entry, and limited budgets. Test actual behavior, not only page existence or overflow. Report limitations candidly.

Use the existing isolated checkout; do not create worktrees unless requested. Preserve unrelated work. Keep state generator output and scripts/footer.html in sync. Changes to shared policy or footers should cover home, comparison, policy explanation, state directory, and all state pages.

Distinguish local changes, pushed branches, pull requests, previews, and verified production deployment in reports. Never tell the founder to wait for propagation when changes have not been deployed. Attach any created or updated PR to the task.
