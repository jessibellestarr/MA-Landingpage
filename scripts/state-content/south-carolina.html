Who can authorize cremation?

Sections 32-8-315 and 32-8-320 recognize preneed authorization and set an agent hierarchy. Without preneed authorization, a qualifying designated agent precedes a spouse, with specified separation exceptions, then adult children, parents, and other classes. Equal-priority objections affect who can sign; disputes among equally ranked people go to probate court.

Ask the provider to review existing instructions and confirm your authority. These provisions address cremation; they are not a universal hierarchy for every funeral decision.

Embalming, containers, and timing

Section 32-8-340 prohibits a crematory from rejecting remains because they are unembalmed. It generally requires 24 hours after death before cremation, with a narrow disease-related written-waiver exception. Section 32-8-335 allows an alternative container requirement but prohibits requiring purchase of a casket for viewing or cremation.

Section 32-8-325 requires authorization, death documentation, a burial-transit permit, and a cremation permit. The waiting period alone does not make the cremation ready to proceed.

Source: South Carolina Safe Cremation Act, sections 32-8-315 through 32-8-340.

Moving between states

Confirm the route, release, permits, and receiving provider before arranging transport.

Ask the releasing facility and receiving provider who may collect the person, who obtains each permit, and what preservation, container, and carrier requirements apply at both ends. A memorial in another state does not necessarily involve moving a body there.

This guide does not resolve family transport, home burial, airline transport, or international repatriation for your route. Get route-specific confirmation before collecting or transporting the person.

Planning ahead

Write down your wishes and who should act. Recording preferences and buying a prepaid contract are separate decisions.

  • What is guaranteed, and what could still cost more?
  • Who holds the money or insurance policy?
  • What happens if I cancel, move, or the provider closes?
  • Can my chosen person find the documents quickly?

Use the South Carolina Department of Consumer Affairs preneed resources for contract oversight. General help: 803-734-4200. Preneed concerns belong with Consumer Affairs; funeral-professional licensing concerns belong with LLR.

Words you can use

Please show me the written requirement behind that charge. Is it a law, a cemetery rule, or your policy? What arrangement could meet my needs without it?

Please quote the total for the arrangements I described, including transport, storage, permits, and charges paid to other businesses. Mark anything not yet known.

Read your federal Funeral Rule rights. Federal rules and state requirements can both apply.

Where to take a concern

For funeral-provider licensing concerns, start with the South Carolina Board of Funeral Service. General help: 803-896-4497 or contact.funeral@llr.sc.gov.

Use LLR’s complaint instructions and forms. LLR investigates licensing and administrative violations; it cannot award restitution. The license holder generally receives the complaint and your name, with rare exceptions. For monetary recovery, ask about the appropriate legal route rather than relying on a disciplinary complaint.

For a possible federal Funeral Rule violation, report it to the FTC. Keep price lists, itemized statements, receipts, emails, dates, and the exact explanation you were given. Describe the conduct and requested resolution without assuming that a high price proves a violation.
