A death has happened

  1. Ask the hospital or medical examiner who can authorize release and whether there is a hold.
  2. Request prices for the arrangements you want. Ask about transport, refrigeration, permits, and third-party charges.
  3. Get the selections and total in writing before agreeing. Keep a copy of every authorization you sign.

Who can make the decisions?

North Carolina recognizes written disposition instructions and delegations under G.S. 130A-420. Without written authorization, priority starts with a surviving spouse, then a majority of reasonably locatable adult children, then surviving parents, then a majority of reasonably locatable adult siblings. Other classes, waivers, and timing provisions follow. An unmarried partner does not automatically have a spouse’s priority.

Bring existing instructions, contracts, and appointment documents to the provider. For a disagreement, ask which provision establishes authority before signing.

Sources: G.S. 130A-420; S.L. 2026-48, section 64, updates the authorization reference.

Refrigeration and cremation timing

Under G.S. 90-210.27A(l), a funeral establishment or reduction facility must refrigerate unembalmed remains at 40°F or below unless final disposition occurs within 24 hours of taking custody. That is a facility-custody clock, not a universal requirement to embalm after death.

G.S. 90-210.129(e) generally bars cremation or alkaline hydrolysis within 24 hours after death, with a narrow disease-related written-waiver exception. A signed authorization is required under G.S. 90-210.125. Meeting a waiting period alone does not clear a medical-examiner hold or complete the paperwork.

Source: S.L. 2026-48, sections 25, 43 and 47; generally effective October 1, 2026 under section 70.

Moving between states

Confirm the route, release, permits, and receiving provider before arranging transport.

G.S. 130A-113 requires an originating jurisdiction’s burial-transit or disposal permit for a body brought into North Carolina. Removal from North Carolina also requires a burial-transit permit. Medical-examiner cases have additional requirements.

Source: G.S. 130A-113.

Ask the releasing facility and receiving provider who may collect the person, who obtains each permit, and what preservation, container, and carrier requirements apply. This guide does not resolve family transport, home burial, airline transport, or international repatriation for your route.

Planning ahead

Write down your wishes and who should act. Recording preferences and buying a prepaid contract are separate decisions. Ask the Board about preneed oversight before paying.

  • What is guaranteed, and what could still cost more?
  • Who holds the money or insurance policy?
  • What happens if I cancel, move, or the provider closes?
  • Can my chosen person find the documents quickly?

Words you can use

Please show me the written requirement behind that charge. Is it a law, a cemetery rule, or your policy? What arrangement could meet my needs without it?

Please quote the total for the arrangements I described, including transport, storage, permits, and charges paid to other businesses. Mark anything not yet known.

Where to take a concern

For funeral-provider or preneed concerns, use the North Carolina Board complaint form. General help: 919-733-9380. The Board does not accept verbal phone complaints. It discloses your identity to the respondent and does not process anonymous complaints. It cannot decide civil liability or give legal advice. Do not wait for its decision before seeking advice about a legal deadline.

Cemetery complaints go to the North Carolina Cemetery Commission, not the funeral Board. Source: Board complaint instructions.

For a possible federal Funeral Rule violation, report it to the FTC. Keep price lists, itemized statements, receipts, emails, dates, and the exact explanation you were given. Describe the conduct and requested resolution without assuming that a high price proves a violation.
