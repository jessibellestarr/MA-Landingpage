A death has happened

  1. Confirm who is legally authorized to direct disposition and whether a coroner, medical examiner, or other authority has placed a hold.

  3. Keep every authorization and ask for the source of anything presented as mandatory.

The funeral establishment must be licensed

Georgia’s State Board of Funeral Service licenses and regulates funeral establishments, crematories, funeral directors, and embalmers. Current Board rules require each funeral establishment or crematory to hold a valid license and operate under a funeral director in full and continuous charge.

Sources: Georgia State Board of Funeral Service; Georgia Rules, Chapter 250-6.

Release to the legally authorized person

Georgia Board Rule 250-6-.09 requires the funeral director in full and continuous charge to ensure that deceased human bodies are released to the legally authorized person upon that person’s request, in accordance with the cited Georgia statutes. The same rule requires proper identification tagging before interment, cremation, or organic human reduction.

Source: Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 250-6-.09.

Crematory oversight

Georgia requires crematories to be separately licensed, and Board rules provide for regular inspections. The Board’s rules also require certified crematory operators before employees operate cremation equipment.

Source: Georgia Rules, Chapter 250-6.

Planning ahead

Recording your wishes and prepaying are separate decisions. Georgia’s funeral-service regulator also oversees laws and rules affecting funeral and cemetery services. Before paying in advance, ask what is guaranteed, who holds the funds or insurance, and what happens if you cancel, move, or the provider closes.

Where to take a concern

The Georgia State Board of Funeral Service accepts licensing complaints through the Secretary of State’s online system and asks complainants to provide as much detail and supporting documentation as possible. Georgia states that licensing complaints are confidential under state law.
