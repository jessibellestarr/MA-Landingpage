Your state starting point

Virginia

Funeral arrangements, planning ahead, and questions to take to the right office.

State-specific legal review pending

This page provides general decision support. It is not a researched summary of Virginia law. Confirm requirements with the relevant authorities before arranging transport or relying on a legal requirement.

A death has happened

  1. Confirm who has authority to arrange services and when the person can be released from the hospital, medical examiner, or other custodian.
  2. Ask for the General Price List and a written, itemized estimate before choosing a package.
  3. Explain your budget and preferences. Ask which charges are optional and what each stated requirement is based on.
  4. Confirm any time-sensitive storage, release, and transport arrangements with the provider.

Planning ahead

  1. Write down your wishes, budget, and who should be contacted. Compare equivalent services from more than one provider.
  2. Separate recording your preferences from paying in advance. You can explore choices without purchasing a contract.
  3. Before prepaying, ask who holds the funds, what is guaranteed, and how cancellation, refunds, relocation, or a provider closure are handled.
  4. Check licensing and preneed oversight with the relevant state authority. Keep copies where your chosen contact can find them.

More than one state may matter

Where you live does not automatically determine all the rules. The provider’s location, where the death occurred, the location of a viewing or funeral, and where burial or cremation occurs can each matter.

A memorial held in another state does not necessarily mean the person who died is being transported there. Transporting a body and transporting cremated remains also involve different questions.

Crossing state lines or arranging family transport

If transport may cross state lines, check the requirements at both ends and any applicable transit requirements before making arrangements.

Family-arranged transport may be possible in some circumstances. This page does not establish that it is permitted for your route. A refrigerated vehicle alone does not establish permission, and we do not claim one is universally required.

Before collecting or transporting the person who died, ask the releasing facility and the relevant vital-records offices or regulators:

  • Who may authorize release, collect the person, and perform the transport?
  • Which death-registration, removal, transit, or disposition permits are needed, and who can obtain them?
  • What container, vehicle, preservation, refrigeration, timing, or public-health requirements apply?
  • Are there medical-examiner holds or other restrictions?
  • Will the destination accept delivery from a family member, and what documents and arrangements does it require?
