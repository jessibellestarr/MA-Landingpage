Embalming is not required by Tennessee law

The Tennessee Attorney General’s consumer guidance states that embalming is not required under Tennessee law. It also says a funeral service provider must disclose that fact in writing, cannot embalm without permission, and must identify required purchases in writing when embalming is required for a selected service.

Source: Tennessee Attorney General: What to Know about Making End-of-Life Plans.

Cremation requires authorization

Tennessee’s Board publishes a cremation authorization form that treats cremation as an irreversible legal act and requires the authorizing agent to certify their authority and identify relevant surviving heirs. If the signer is not the legal next of kin or not all people of the same degree are signing, the form calls for a written explanation.

Source: Tennessee Board pre-approved cremation authorization form.

Planning ahead

Tennessee regulates funeral establishments under Title 62, Chapter 5 and regulates cemeteries and burial services through the Department of Commerce and Insurance. The state also regulates preneed sellers and sales agents. Before paying in advance, ask what is guaranteed, who holds the money or policy, and how cancellation, transfer, or provider closure works.

Source: Tennessee Board of Funeral Directors and Embalmers/Burial Services.

Where to take a concern

The Tennessee Board encourages consumers to file complaints about suspected misconduct, unlicensed activity, or unfair or deceptive practices. The Board says it cannot give legal advice or represent a consumer seeking a refund or reimbursement.

File a Tennessee funeral or burial-services complaint · Board: 615-741-5062.

For a possible federal Funeral Rule violation, report it to the FTC.
